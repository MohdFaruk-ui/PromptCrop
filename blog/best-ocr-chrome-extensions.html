5 Best OCR Chrome Extensions in 2026 (Compared)

By FarKode • Last Updated: June 20, 2026 • 12 min read

Choosing the best OCR Chrome extension in 2026 is harder than it sounds. There are dozens of options in the Chrome Web Store, but they all make the same promise: "extract text from images." The real differences are in how they do it — and what they do with your data behind the scenes.

We built SnapTextify to solve the pain of copying text from videos, but we know there are other tools out there. To help you choose the right fit, we did a technical side-by-side comparison of the most popular OCR extensions in the Chrome Web Store. Below is a breakdown of how they compare on speed, privacy architecture, accuracy, and developer workflows.

Here's what we found. No marketing fluff — just a technical breakdown of what works and what doesn't.

#1

SnapTextify

100% offline OCR powered by local WebAssembly — no data ever leaves your browser.

SnapTextify is the only extension on this list that processes everything entirely offline. Your screenshots never get uploaded anywhere. The OCR engine (Tesseract, compiled to WebAssembly) runs right inside your browser tab — no server calls, no API keys, no cloud processing.

The workflow is dead simple: press Alt + C, drag a box around any text on your screen, and it's copied to your clipboard. The whole thing takes about 300 milliseconds. It works on YouTube videos, web images, PDFs rendered in the browser, Figma designs — basically anything visible on your screen.

It auto-detects 9 languages without you needing to configure anything — English, Hindi, Bengali, Spanish, German, French, Portuguese, Russian, and Arabic. For most users, you never even touch the settings.

The free tier gives you 3 scans per day. If you need more, paid plans start at ₹149 for 300 scans (Starter), ₹399 for 1,500 scans (Pro), or ₹799 for lifetime unlimited access.

Strengths

  • 100% offline — true zero-data-upload privacy
  • Extremely fast (~300ms extraction)
  • Works on video frames (YouTube, Udemy, etc.)
  • 9-language auto-detection out of the box
  • Clean keyboard shortcut workflow
  • Affordable lifetime plan available

Limitations

  • Free tier limited to 3 scans per day
  • 9 languages vs. 100+ on cloud tools
  • No built-in translation feature
  • Chrome-only (no Firefox version yet)
#2

Project Naptha

Makes text inside web images selectable, highlightable, and searchable — like native text.

Project Naptha takes a unique approach. Instead of giving you a "capture" tool, it automatically detects text inside images on any web page and makes that text selectable — just like regular HTML text. You can highlight it, copy it, even search for it.

The concept is genuinely clever. When it works, it feels like magic. You hover over a meme, a screenshot, or an infographic, and suddenly the text is selectable.

The problem? It doesn't work consistently in 2026. The extension hasn't received major updates recently, and it struggles with modern web apps, video frames, and dynamically loaded content. It also can't handle text on top of complex backgrounds or gradients particularly well.

It does do some processing locally, but it also communicates with external servers for certain operations — so it's not fully offline.

Strengths

  • Unique "selectable text in images" approach
  • Completely free to use
  • No manual triggering needed — works automatically
  • Some processing happens locally

Limitations

  • Inconsistent on modern web apps
  • Doesn't work on video frames
  • Not fully offline — some server calls
  • Development has slowed significantly
  • Struggles with complex image backgrounds
#3

Copyfish

Cloud-based OCR with built-in translation, designed for multilingual text extraction.

Copyfish has been around for years and it's still one of the most well-known OCR extensions. It lets you draw a selection rectangle over any part of your screen, sends that region to a cloud OCR engine (you can choose between free OCR.space API or Google Cloud Vision), and returns the extracted text.

The big selling point is its integration with translation. After extracting text, you can immediately translate it into another language — which makes it genuinely useful for people reading foreign-language websites, manga, or subtitles.

The trade-off is clear: your screenshots are uploaded to a cloud server for processing. If you're working with personal or proprietary content, that's something to think about. The free tier is also fairly limited — you'll likely need to pay for the Pro version if you use it regularly.

Strengths

  • Built-in translation feature
  • Supports 100+ languages
  • Choice of OCR engines (OCR.space or Google)
  • Available on Chrome, Firefox, and Edge
  • Mature product with years of development

Limitations

  • Screenshots uploaded to cloud — privacy concern
  • Slower than offline solutions (2-5 second round trip)
  • Free tier is restrictive
  • Requires internet connection
  • Occasional errors during peak usage
#4

Google Lens (Chrome built-in)

Google's integrated visual search and OCR, built right into Chrome's right-click menu.

If you're running Chrome, you already have Google Lens. Right-click on any image (or use "Search with Google Lens" from the context menu) and it'll analyze the image — including extracting any text it finds.

Google Lens is impressive in terms of raw OCR accuracy. It handles messy handwriting, complex layouts, and obscure languages better than most standalone tools. It also lets you translate, search, or copy extracted text.

The catch? Everything goes through Google's servers. Your image is uploaded to Google for processing, and it becomes part of your Google activity history. If you value privacy — or if you're working with sensitive screenshots — this is a significant consideration.

Also, Google Lens doesn't work well for the specific use case of extracting text from video frames while watching. You'd have to screenshot first, then right-click the image. It's not a one-step workflow.

Strengths

  • Already built into Chrome — no installation
  • Excellent accuracy (Google's Vision AI)
  • Handles handwriting and complex layouts
  • 100+ language support
  • Completely free

Limitations

  • All images processed by Google's cloud
  • Data becomes part of your Google activity
  • Not optimized for video frame extraction
  • Multi-step workflow (screenshot → right-click)
  • Requires internet connection
#5

Easy Screen OCR

Screenshot-to-text tool with cloud-powered OCR and a simple capture interface.

Easy Screen OCR positions itself as the simplest path from screenshot to text. You capture a region of your screen, it uploads the image to its cloud OCR service, and returns the extracted text. It also has a companion desktop app for users who want OCR outside the browser.

The interface is clean and beginner-friendly. It supports a wide range of languages and handles common use cases — documents, slides, receipts — reasonably well.

The downsides mirror other cloud-based tools: screenshots leave your machine, processing is slower than local OCR, and the free tier is limited. The desktop app offers more features but comes with a separate subscription.

Strengths

  • Simple and beginner-friendly interface
  • Companion desktop app available
  • Supports many languages
  • Handles documents and receipts well

Limitations

  • Screenshots uploaded to cloud for processing
  • Slower processing (3-6 seconds)
  • Free tier is restrictive
  • Desktop app requires separate purchase
  • Less reliable on code and terminal text

So, Which OCR Chrome Extension Should You Pick?

There's no single "best" extension for everyone. It depends on what you prioritize:

  • Privacy and speed are non-negotiable? → SnapTextify is the clear winner. It's the only tool here that runs 100% offline with zero data uploads. If you work with sensitive code, internal dashboards, or proprietary content, this is the safe choice.
  • Need translation alongside OCR? → Copyfish is purpose-built for this. If you're regularly reading content in foreign languages, the integrated translation workflow saves time.
  • Want the best raw accuracy on messy text? → Google Lens has the most advanced OCR engine. For handwritten notes, scanned documents, or tricky layouts, it's hard to beat Google's Vision AI — if you're comfortable with the privacy trade-off.
  • Love the idea of selectable text in images? → Project Naptha is still conceptually brilliant, but its reliability in 2026 has declined. Worth trying, but have a backup.
  • Just need something simple for occasional screenshots? → Easy Screen OCR or even Google Lens will get the job done without overthinking it.

Our recommendation for developers: If you watch tutorial videos, work with code, or handle anything remotely sensitive, SnapTextify's offline-first approach makes the most sense. The Alt + C shortcut becomes second nature, and you never have to worry about what happens to your screenshots.
